## Observed Behavior

The variant is a minimal positional grid envelope:

- Envelope shape is `[pcid, payload]`.
- Encoding is deterministic CBOR.
- Receivers hard-reject envelopes whose `pcid` handler is unknown.
- The base envelope has no v0 signature requirement; authorship, integrity, and accountability are delegated to the carrying transport or the payload protocol named by `pcid`.

Against `shipping-label-printing`, this behaves as a narrow dispatch wrapper rather than a complete application substrate. It can carry shipping-label payload bytes if Alice, Bob, Carol, and any necessary carrier or auditor already know the relevant pCID handler and the payload protocol supplies signatures, authorization semantics, payment evidence, address-data handling, and chain-of-custody records.

The hard-reject unknown-pCID policy is the strongest observable pressure. It fails closed when Bob or Carol receives an envelope for an unknown shipping-label, carrier, payment, or custody payload protocol. That is good for avoiding accidental acceptance of uninterpretable messages as valid evidence, but it is weak for sparse logistics workflows where intermediaries may need to store, forward, escrow, or later audit opaque payloads without understanding every application protocol.

The unsigned-v0 policy keeps the envelope small but pushes all application-critical accountability out of the envelope. For shipping-label printing, that means label authorization, payment authorization, custody handoff, address correction, and later dispute evidence must all be expressed inside the payload protocol or in transport-level evidence. If those layers are well designed, the envelope can remain a simple dispatch boundary. If they are absent or inconsistent, this variant provides no envelope-level help.

## Verdict

Evidence verdict: partial fit, likely negative-control value.

This variant is useful as a minimal baseline for comparing grid-envelope siblings. It is not a strong standalone fit for `shipping-label-printing` because the scenario needs long-lived, auditable, cross-party evidence for carrier selection, label authorization, payment, address data, and chain of custody. The variant deliberately does not provide envelope-level signatures or opaque unknown-pCID forwarding, so application success depends on external payload protocols and pCID distribution/migration practices.

The strongest argument for the variant is simplicity and explicit failure: unknown handlers fail visibly at the envelope dispatch boundary, and the envelope does not pretend to authenticate what it cannot interpret. The strongest argument against it is operational brittleness: over a 100-year, sparse-knowledge logistics setting, hard rejection can prevent useful custody, forwarding, and later audit of still-opaque evidence, while unsigned envelopes force every application payload to solve accountability independently.
