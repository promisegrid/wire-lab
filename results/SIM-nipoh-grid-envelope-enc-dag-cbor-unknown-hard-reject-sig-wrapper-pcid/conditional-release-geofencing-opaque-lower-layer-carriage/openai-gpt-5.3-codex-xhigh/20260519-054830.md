## Observed Behavior

- **What this simulation clearly covers**
  - It provides a strict envelope dispatch boundary via `pcid`.
  - It allows opaque payload bytes at the envelope level (envelope does not require semantic understanding of payload).
  - It fail-closes on unknown top-level `pcid`, preventing accidental interpretation/acceptance of unsupported protocols.

- **What this simulation pushes to another layer**
  - Condition vocabulary semantics (including geofencing/conditional-release meaning) are not defined by this envelope.
  - Verification of condition objects is delegated to payload protocol(s), not solved by envelope itself.
  - Authorship/integrity are delegated to wrapper protocols selected by `pcid` (no fixed base signature slot).
  - pCID discovery/governance and migration coordination are not specified (explicitly non-goal for central registry).

- **Scenario-specific behavior under two practical cases**
  - If Bob knows the outer carrier `pcid` but cannot parse inner condition vocabulary bytes: he can carry/store envelope bytes as opaque payload under that known protocol, but cannot independently validate condition semantics at envelope layer.
  - If Bob receives an envelope whose top-level `pcid` is unknown (e.g., new condition-carriage protocol): hard-reject policy blocks treating it as valid and forbids accept/store/forward as valid envelope, prioritizing safety over interoperability.

- **Net behavioral posture**
  - The specimen is strong on “do not silently misinterpret unknown protocol.”
  - It is weak on “let lower layers continue opaque carriage across unknown future protocol variants” unless compatibility is pre-arranged through known wrappers.

## Verdict

Evidence verdict: Partial fit — this specimen demonstrates safe fail-closed dispatch boundaries, but it does not by itself satisfy the scenario’s requirement for durable opaque lower-layer carriage plus condition verifiability across sparse, evolving, no-central-authority environments.

- **100-year durability:** Weak/partial. Hard reject plus unfrozen draft status means long-horizon interoperability depends on continual handler rollout or stable compatibility wrappers not yet specified.
- **Sparse, partial knowledge:** Mixed. Opaque payload carriage helps when the outer `pcid` is known, but unknown top-level `pcid` handling is brittle under partial deployment.
- **No central authority:** Mixed-positive. It avoids mandating a central registry, but leaves unresolved how decentralized peers converge on trusted `pcid`/handler mappings over time.
- **Human and LLM auditability:** Mixed. Byte-level reproducibility is strong (canonical DAG-CBOR positional array), but semantic audit of promises depends on obtaining and understanding external wrapper/payload protocols.
- **Migration pressure:** Weak. Unknown-hard-reject creates upgrade cliffs; no explicit migration profile (e.g., quarantine carriage, dual-wrap compatibility, negotiated downgrade/forwarding rules) is provided.

Overall, the simulation is a good safety-focused envelope primitive, but insufficient as complete evidence for this scenario without additional protocol commitments above/beside the envelope.
